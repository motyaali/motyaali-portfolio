The operating problem

A workflow fails when people know the policy but cannot see their next action.

Complex document processes depend on contributors understanding the correct sequence, role, record location, naming and metadata requirements, review authority, and publication condition. Fragmented instructions create rework, inconsistent routing, permission problems, and uncertainty about ownership.

My work translates process requirements into usable guidance, visual workflow logic, support records, and training materials that help people complete the work correctly.

Before and after

What the documentation changes.

The value is reduced ambiguity at the moment a person must act.

Before

Instructions are fragmented by system or policy source.

Users search for the right procedure, interpret their role, guess the sequence, encounter permission issues, and rely on informal follow-up to determine what happened next.

After

The user sees the role, required inputs, next action, and handoff.

Guidance connects the governing process to the actual operating sequence, while document control preserves status, revision, access, and final record history.

Selected design decisions

Documentation quality is an operating control.

  • Organize by user role and action rather than by software menu.
  • Separate preparation, review, approval, and publication authority.
  • Place required metadata and completeness checks before routing.
  • Show the expected handoff and the condition that starts the next stage.
  • Record permission and identity issues as workflow blockers, not isolated help requests.
  • Preserve revision and disposition history after the work is complete.
