Construction Project Coordination Controls

A reconstructed urban transit rehabilitation case study showing how I organize scope, procurement, change documentation, meetings, financial records, schedules, and closeout while working within established procedures and approval authority.

Review the case study Back to Work

Purpose

This project is designed to demonstrate readiness for project coordination and construction administration work. It does not propose replacing an employer's systems or procedures. The operating principle is simple: learn the approved process, execute it accurately, keep ownership and status visible, escalate exceptions with supporting facts, and suggest small improvements only after understanding the team's needs.

Evidence boundary

The case study is reconstructed from construction administration coursework and expanded using my current understanding of document control, workflow coordination, financial tracking, and human review. Project names, dates, costs, vendors, and records used in the final artifacts will be fictional or synthetic. No confidential employer, client, or contractor material will be published.

Scenario

A public transit agency is preparing a rehabilitation project involving removal and replacement of structural street gratings in a dense urban environment. The work requires clear scope definition, controlled procurement, documented changes, coordinated meetings, accurate project data, invoice review support, schedule visibility, and complete turnover records.

My role in the demonstration

Project Coordinator responsible for organizing the administrative flow of information. The role does not independently approve contracts, design changes, payments, or technical decisions. It prepares complete records, routes information to the correct reviewers, tracks status and deadlines, documents decisions, and keeps project managers informed of missing information or emerging risks.

Coordination lifecycle

  1. Review source requirements. Identify the governing drawings, specifications, scope boundaries, deadlines, and required approvals.
  2. Prepare and track solicitation records. Organize quote requests, bidder communications, required documents, questions, addenda, and submission status.
  3. Coordinate commitments and changes. Record the source, description, cost and schedule impact, supporting documents, owner, review status, and next action.
  4. Support meetings and communications. Separate discussion, decisions, actions, owners, and due dates so meeting records support execution.
  5. Maintain project data and financial controls. Verify required fields, supporting documentation, revised commitments, invoice status, discrepancies, and approval routing.
  6. Prepare for closeout throughout the project. Track turnover requirements, warranties, final changes, outstanding documents, financial reconciliation, and archive readiness.

Four control lenses

The case study applies a framework developed through my construction administration coursework: project decisions must balance time, money, safety, and quality. Administrative work supports all four by preserving accurate records, making deadlines and ownership visible, documenting changes, and ensuring required reviews are completed.

  • Time: milestones, dependencies, action owners, due dates, and delay visibility
  • Money: commitments, quotations, approved changes, invoices, balances, and reconciliation
  • Safety: required plans, approvals, meeting actions, documentation, and escalation of missing controls
  • Quality: revision control, required attachments, source references, complete records, and closeout readiness

Controls built into the documents

The artifacts are designed to fit within an established project management system. The improvements are small, practical controls rather than a replacement workflow.

  • Required owner, status, next action, and due date fields
  • Consistent status definitions and revision references
  • Missing-document and overdue-item flags
  • Links between changes, commitments, invoices, and closeout records
  • Documented approval history and escalation reason
  • Formula and completeness checks before routing
