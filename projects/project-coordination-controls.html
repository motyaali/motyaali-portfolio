Construction Project Coordination Controls

A complete urban infrastructure scenario showing how I organize scope, procurement, change documentation, meetings, financial records, schedules, and closeout while respecting established procedures and approval authority.

Open the evidence dashboard View the connected process

Purpose

This case study demonstrates how I would support project teams by maintaining accurate records, visible ownership, complete routing, timely follow-through, and decision-ready status. The controls are platform-neutral and intended to work within an organization's approved procedures rather than replace them.

Disclosure

All project names, organizations, dates, costs, vendors, and records are fictional. The case demonstrates my approach to project coordination and construction administration. It does not represent confidential client work or independent contract, technical, financial, or payment authority.

Scenario

A fictional public transit agency is delivering a structural street-grating rehabilitation project in a dense urban environment. The project requires clear scope definition, controlled procurement, documented changes, coordinated meetings, accurate project data, invoice review support, schedule visibility, and complete turnover records.

Project Coordinator role

The Project Coordinator prepares, verifies, routes, tracks, documents, and escalates. Authorized project personnel retain responsibility for technical direction, contract decisions, commercial approvals, payments, and final acceptance.

What the evidence demonstrates

  • Project baselines that define scope, exclusions, milestones, stakeholders, and authority before tracking begins
  • Dashboards that make risks, owners, next actions, deadlines, and decision needs visible
  • Procurement records that connect solicitation requirements, bidder submissions, evaluation status, and award communication
  • Meeting records that separate discussion, decisions, actions, owners, authority, and due dates
  • Change-package controls that link source conditions, technical records, quotations, cost, time, safety, and routing
  • Invoice controls that reconcile commitments and billing while holding incomplete support before approval routing
  • Closeout tracking that begins during delivery rather than at the end of the project

Coordination lifecycle

  1. Review source requirements. Identify governing documents, scope boundaries, deadlines, and approvals.
  2. Prepare and track solicitation records. Organize quote requests, bidder communications, addenda, and submission status.
  3. Record the authorized commitment. Link the selected bid, contract, insurance, schedule baseline, and Notice to Proceed.
  4. Support meetings and communications. Record decisions, actions, owners, authority, and due dates.
  5. Coordinate potential changes. Connect the source, technical record, quotation, cost, time, safety, reviewers, and disposition.
  6. Maintain project and financial data. Verify required fields, revised commitments, invoice support, discrepancies, and routing.
  7. Manage closeout readiness. Track turnover records, warranties, final changes, reconciliation, and archive status.

Four control lenses

The case evaluates project information through four connected lenses:

Time

Milestones, dependencies, action owners, due dates, delays, and schedule effects.

Money

Commitments, quotations, approved changes, invoices, retainage, balances, and reconciliation.

Safety

Required plans, pedestrian controls, work-hour constraints, documentation, and unresolved controls.

Quality

Revision control, required attachments, source references, complete records, and turnover readiness.

Practical controls

  • One current owner, status, next action, due date, and escalation trigger
  • Consistent identifiers, status definitions, and revision references
  • Missing-document and routing-gate flags before approval begins
  • Linked procurement, decision, change, commitment, invoice, schedule, and closeout records
  • Clear separation between administrative verification and approval authority
  • System-of-record updates only after authorized action
