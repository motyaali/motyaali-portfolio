Evaluated IBM SkillsBuild Capstone & Case Study

Unseen Lifeline

An accessibility-centered human-AI workflow for turning an urgent institutional notice into verified requirements, evidence needs, deadlines, communications, and a sequenced response plan.

The problem

People can lose rights, services, or opportunities because a process is difficult to navigate.

High-stakes notices often combine dense language, strict deadlines, fragmented documentation, multiple agencies, and severe cognitive load. The failure may be procedural rather than a lack of underlying eligibility or merit.

Unseen Lifeline is designed to reduce that burden by separating source evidence from interpretation, making deadlines and missing information visible, preparing draft communications, and keeping final review and submission with the person or their authorized support.

Representative transformation

From an overwhelming notice to a reviewable response plan.

The fictional example below demonstrates the structure without exposing personal records or representing legal advice.

Incoming notice

“Provide the requested work-activity forms and supporting records within 10 days.”

The notice includes multiple forms, a response deadline, unfamiliar terminology, possible benefit consequences, and no organized list of what evidence already exists.

Prepared response view

One deadline, four evidence needs, three open questions, and a sequenced action plan.

The workflow extracts the stated requirement, records the source language, identifies missing records, prepares questions for the agency, drafts a good-cause explanation for review, and creates a submission checklist.

Workflow architecture

Source first, then requirements, evidence, review, and action.

The process is designed to preserve uncertainty and prevent a draft from being mistaken for an approved submission.

01

Preserve source

Retain the original notice and identify the issuing organization, date, and stated consequence.

02

Decompose

Separate requirements, deadlines, forms, evidence requests, contacts, and unclear terms.

03

Compare evidence

Mark what exists, what is missing, what conflicts, and what requires verification.

04

Prepare support

Draft questions, communication, accommodation requests, checklists, and an action sequence.

05

Human decision

Review accuracy, obtain advice where needed, approve the final response, submit, and retain proof.

Selected design decisions

Accessibility is part of the operating architecture.

  • Preserve the exact source language before simplifying it.
  • Separate confirmed facts, inferred meaning, open questions, and draft language.
  • Make the smallest next action visible instead of presenting one large response task.
  • Track deadlines with their source and calculation assumptions.
  • Return uncertainty to the user rather than silently resolving it.
  • Keep legal, medical, financial, benefits, and eligibility judgment with qualified people.

Current maturity and boundaries

Unseen Lifeline is an evaluated capstone, product concept, and structured case study. It is not a deployed autonomous platform and does not determine eligibility, provide professional advice, or submit information on a person's behalf. Any real use would require source verification, privacy controls, qualified review where appropriate, and explicit user approval.
